# ADR-012: One home for each kind of money

Status: Accepted, feat/treasury · Date: October 9, 2026

## Context

Section 3.1 and 4.5 already named `Staking` and `Treasury` as separate contracts with separate jobs, but `Staking` as built in `feat/staking` (PR #7) could not yet call a real `Treasury`, since `Treasury` did not exist. It held a stand-in for the reward side of that split directly: a local `RewardPool` (instance) and `AccruedReward(addr)` balances that `fund_rewards`/`claim_rewards` moved, alongside the protocol's slashed-funds half of every forfeited bond and slash, which was credited straight into a `Claimable(addr)` balance naming the configured `treasury` address, rather than actually reaching a contract there.

Building `Treasury` (`feat/treasury`) raised the question Section 4.5's split had always implied but never enforced: now that a real `Treasury` contract exists, should `Staking` keep crediting protocol funds into its own storage at all, or should every protocol-destined amount leave `Staking` immediately, the moment it is decided, through a real cross-contract call?

## Decision

**Each kind of money has exactly one home, and it is a kind that is checked by test, not merely documented:**

- **`Staking` holds only participant funds**: keeper bonds, reporter stakes (including stake in cooldown, still fully slashable per ADR-011), locked dispute bonds, and `Claimable(addr)` balances owed to a specific participant (refunds, winnings). Every one of these is, in principle, paid out to the single address whose funds they are.
- **`Treasury` holds only protocol funds**, in the four `TreasuryBucket`s Section 12.7 already named: `Fees`, `Slashed`, `KeeperRewards`, `ReporterRewards`. None of these belongs to any one participant until `Treasury.accrue_reward` names a recipient; even then, the funds leave `Staking`'s side of the ledger at the moment they are decided, not at the moment they are claimed.

This is enforced structurally, not by convention: `Staking` has no code path left that credits `Claimable` with a protocol-destined amount. Every protocol-destined credit, the treasury's half of a `forfeit_bond` or `slash`, now calls a new `deposit_treasury_share` helper, which makes a real `Treasury.deposit(Staking, Slashed, amount)` call rather than writing to `Staking`'s own storage. Reporter and keeper rewards are accrued directly against `Treasury.accrue_reward`, never against a `Staking`-local pool: `RewardPool`, `AccruedReward(addr)`, `fund_rewards` and `claim_rewards` are removed from `Staking` entirely, not merely deprecated alongside the new path.

The two invariants this produces are each checked by a dedicated test, not asserted only in prose:

- **Staking's own balance invariant** (tightened from `>=` to exact `==`, apart from direct donations): `Staking`'s USDC balance equals keeper bonds + reporter stakes (including cooldown) + locked bonds + `Claimable` balances, exactly. `Staking` holding one stroop more than this sum, once direct donations are excluded, would mean some protocol-destined amount never actually left.
- **Treasury's own balance invariant (T1)**: `Treasury`'s USDC balance is at least the sum of its four bucket balances plus every accrued, unclaimed reward (`>=`, since a direct transfer to `Treasury` counts as a donation, not a violation).

A call that pays a protocol-destined amount to `Staking`'s own `treasury` config address but never reaches a real `Treasury.deposit` call is a bug under this ADR, not an acceptable stand-in, regardless of whether the funds are provably recoverable some other way.

## Consequences

- `deposit_treasury_share(env, config, amount)` makes a real, nested cross-contract call: `Staking` (not a human signer) is the `from` in the `usdc.transfer(Staking, Treasury, amount)` call `Treasury.deposit` itself performs. Soroban's own auth-depth rule (a contract's own direct calls are auto-authorized, but a callee's own further call back to the caller's `require_auth()` is not, two hops deep) means this needs `env.authorize_as_current_contract(...)`, naming the exact sub-invocation `Treasury.deposit` will make into `usdc.transfer`, called by `Staking` before the `Treasury.deposit` call itself. Verified against a standalone scratch test under both mocked and fully unmocked auth checking before landing in `Staking`.
- `Staking.reward_keeper` and `settle_probes`'s reporter-reward accrual both call `Treasury.accrue_reward` directly; neither keeps or checks any local balance of its own. A short `Treasury` bucket caps the accrual and emits `RewardShortfall` (new, Section 13); `Staking` does not duplicate that capping or that event.
- `Staking`'s `treasury: Address` config field, set at `initialize`, must now be a real deployed `Treasury` contract for any call that reaches `deposit_treasury_share`, `Treasury.accrue_reward`, or a keeper's or reporter's `Treasury.claim_reward` to succeed; a placeholder address (as `feat/staking`'s own pre-`Treasury` tests used, and as the task brief's earlier integration test still does for its own narrower, no-slash scenario) only remains safe for a scenario that never exercises one of those calls.
- Sections touched: technical-doc.md 4.5, 7.5, 7.8, 12.3, 12.7, 13, 14, 15.1, 23, 24.2.
